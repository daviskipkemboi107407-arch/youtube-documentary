# Research Standard
## Purpose
Every documentary must be built on reliable evidence, clear reasoning, and honest treatment of uncertainty.
## Story Selection
A potential story should have:
- A compelling central question
- Genuine historical, scientific, or investigative interest
- Enough reliable evidence to research properly
- A strong narrative or mystery
- Something meaningful for the viewer to discover
- Potential for strong visual storytelling
Avoid stories that depend primarily on rumors, fabricated claims, conspiracy theories presented as fact, or weak evidence.
## Source Priority
Prefer sources in roughly this order:
1. Primary sources
2. Academic papers and research institutions
3. Museums, archives, universities, and government institutions
4. Reputable books and scholarly publications
5. Established journalism
6. Secondary sources used for leads and context
A source should not be treated as reliable simply because it appears high in search results.
## Verification
Important claims should be checked against multiple independent sources whenever practical.
Distinguish clearly between:
- Confirmed facts
- Strongly supported conclusions
- Plausible explanations
- Disputed claims
- Unknown or unresolved questions
Never present speculation as established fact.
## Research Notes
For each documentary, record:
- Claim
- Source
- Relevant evidence
- Source reliability
- Conflicting information
- Level of certainty
- Notes for the script
## AI Usage
AI may assist with:
- Finding research leads
- Organizing information
- Summarizing sources
- Generating research questions
- Identifying gaps or contradictions
AI must not be treated as the final authority for factual claims.
Important claims must be verified against actual sources.
## Originality
The documentary should add original value through:
- Research
- Story structure
- Analysis
- Connections between evidence
- Clear explanations
- Strong narration
Do not simply rewrite another creator's documentary.
