# Story Evaluation System
## Purpose
Evaluate documentary ideas before committing significant research or production time.
## Scoring
Score each category from 1 to 10.
### 1. Curiosity
Does the idea immediately create a strong question?
### 2. Evidence
Is there enough reliable evidence to support a documentary?
### 3. Narrative
Can the story develop naturally with tension, discoveries, revelations, or competing explanations?
### 4. Originality
Can we approach the subject with an original angle rather than simply repeating existing documentaries?
### 5. Visual Potential
Can the story be communicated through compelling photographs, maps, documents, footage, graphics, diagrams, animation, or other legitimate visual assets?
### 6. Audience Appeal
Would a broad audience reasonably want to know the answer?
### 7. Factual Confidence
How confidently can the major claims be verified?
### 8. Production Feasibility
Can the documentary be produced to a high standard with the resources available?
## Score
Maximum score: 80
Total Score = Curiosity + Evidence + Narrative + Originality + Visual Potential + Audience Appeal + Factual Confidence + Production Feasibility
## Decision Guidelines
### 70–80
Exceptional candidate.
Strongly consider for production.
### 60–69
Strong candidate.
Research further before committing.
### 50–59
Potential candidate.
Requires a particularly strong angle or additional evidence.
### Below 50
Do not prioritize.
## Red Flags
An idea should be rejected or reconsidered if:
- The central claim cannot be verified
- The story depends mainly on conspiracy theories
- Reliable sources are extremely limited
- The strongest version of the story is demonstrably false
- The story has little meaningful visual potential
- The subject is already heavily covered without a compelling new angle
- Production requirements are unrealistic
- The story requires presenting speculation as fact
## Final Decision
The numerical score is a decision aid, not an automatic approval system.
A high score does not guarantee production.
Human judgment remains responsible for the final decision.
